<h2 id="nfl-radar-scaling" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>Research radar: geometry and evidence limits</h2>

The seven fixed clockwise positions correspond to arm strength, processing, pocket presence, mobility, short accuracy, medium accuracy and deep accuracy. **Arm strength and processing remain not measured.** The other positions show named recorded outcomes where evidence exists. A gap means unavailable evidence, not a low rating. The number of plotted outcomes can differ between the selected player-seasons.

**Dropbacks without a sack (%)** occupies the desired pocket-presence position. **Scramble contribution** occupies the desired mobility position only when the selected ID and season match one of the original 40 audited records from 2023/2024. Other selections show **No audited scramble-value record**. Sack avoidance reflects protection, receiver availability and decisions with other costs. Scramble contribution combines usage with whole-play shared value. Neither isolates the desired trait.

The short, medium and deep positions show **completions above expectation** on throws in each depth bin, in percentage points. These depth-specific completion residuals describe recorded outcomes relative to the supplied model; they do not isolate throwing accuracy or ball placement. <h2 id="nfl-definition-epa-dropback" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>Value per dropback</h2>

Average EPA on this player's recorded passes, sacks and scrambles.

Unit: expected points per recorded dropback. Positive means modeled improvement over the starting situations, not proof of above-league-average or independent QB ability.

<p role="math" aria-label="Value per dropback equals the sum of expected points added on recorded dropbacks divided by the number of those dropbacks.">Value per dropback = sum of dropback EPA ÷ number of recorded dropbacks.</p>

The formula is `sum_D(epa_t)/N_D`. Total EPA is the numerator alone; efficiency and volume answer different questions. [Read which plays count](#nfl-definition-populations), [how expected points work](#nfl-method-models), and [the limitations](#nfl-limitations).

<h2 id="nfl-definition-cpoe" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>Completions above expectation (CPOE)</h2>

How far the player's completion rate finished above or below the model's expectation for the included throws.

<p role="math" aria-label="CPOE equals completion percentage minus expected completion percentage on the same included throws.">CPOE = completion percentage − expected completion percentage.</p>

Unit: percentage points. The supplied play value is `100 × (recorded completion − supplied completion probability)`. The project averages those supplied values across the same included throws used for completion rate. In symbols: `mean_E(cpoe_t) = 100 × sum_E(y_t − p_t) / N_E`, where `y_t` is one for a completion and zero otherwise, and `p_t` is the supplied probability between zero and one. The expected completion rate explains CPOE; it is not an additional player metric.

Zero means matching that model expectation on the included throws, not zero accuracy. Receiver/defender actions, target choices and unmeasured circumstances remain. This does not measure ball placement or all-around accuracy. [Model explanation](#nfl-method-models) · [Included throws](#nfl-definition-populations) · [Limitations](#nfl-limitations).

<h2 id="nfl-definition-depth" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>Completions above expectation by throw depth</h2>

Air yards measure the recorded target depth relative to the line of scrimmage. The short bin includes throws from 0 up to, but not including, 10 air yards: `[0,10)`. Medium uses `[10,20)` and deep uses `[20,70)`. The separate negative-air-yard bin uses `[−15,0)` and remains an optional diagnostic, outside the radar. Boundaries belong to the bin starting at that value; a throw at 10 air yards is medium. These bins do not identify read order, target openness, arm strength or processing.

For a bin `b`, let `E_b` contain that player's included throws in that season and bin, with `N_b` throws. Its completion residual is `theta_b = mean_E_b(cpoe_t) = 100 × sum_E_b(y_t − p_t) / N_b`. Here `y_t` is one for a recorded completion and zero otherwise, and `p_t` is the supplied completion probability. This is a pooled throw-level mean, not an average of game percentages. A value of +3 percentage points means the observed completion rate exceeded the model's expectation by three points on those bin throws.

The annual eligibility threshold applies before depth splitting; the analysis adds no minimum bin-volume rule. Evidence shows the bin's throw count, games containing bin throws, and all included-throw games. An empty bin has no estimate. A failed game-deletion denominator leaves its range unavailable; no failed deletion disappears from the calculation and no unavailable value becomes zero. [Read the game-based calculation](#nfl-method-uncertainty).

<h3 id="nfl-definition-percentage-points" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>Percentage points</h3>

Percentage points are differences between percentages, not percent improvement or a percentile. CPOE subtracts the expected completion percentage from the observed completion percentage on the same throws.

<h3 id="nfl-definition-completion" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>Completion rate</h3>

The percentage of included throws recorded as complete. It uses the same eligible throws as CPOE here, not every official passing attempt. Formula: `100 × recorded completions / included throws`. It remains a diagnostic comparator in the optional research.

<h3 id="nfl-definition-cp" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>Expected completion chance (CP)</h3>

The supplied model's estimated chance that an included throw becomes a completion. Recorded throw context explains some difficulty; the estimate does not measure throwing talent or every influence on the play. CP supplies explanatory background, not another player metric.

<h2 id="nfl-definition-ep" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>Expected points</h2>

The model's value of a game situation, based on chances of the next scoring event in the half. Values are signed from the team-with-possession perspective. This is a model expectation, not a promised score.

Formula: `EP(x) = sum_j[p_j(x) × v_j]`, where `p_j` is the upstream probability of a scoring category and `v_j` its signed scoring value, including zero for no score. [How the supplied models work](#nfl-method-models).

<h2 id="nfl-definition-epa" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>Play value (EPA)</h2>

How much a play improved or worsened the offense's expected points. Expected points added is whole-play shared value, not points personally created by the QB or actual points scored.

<p role="math" aria-label="Expected points added equals the provider's value of the resulting situation minus expected points before the play.">EPA = value of the result − expected points before the play.</p>

The provider values the result after scoring, possession and period-ending handling. On an ordinary non-scoring play with continuing possession within a half, this reduces to expected points after minus expected points before. Turnovers require the original offense's perspective; scoring and end-of-half cases require the provider's rules. The project uses supplied EPA and does not rebuild it by subtracting consecutive play-by-play rows. Zero EPA means no modeled net gain, not zero yards or no scoring. [Model explanation](#nfl-method-models) · [Limitations](#nfl-limitations).

<h2 id="nfl-definition-sack-avoidance" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>Dropbacks without a sack</h2>

The percentage of recorded dropbacks that did not end in a recorded sack.

<p role="math" aria-label="Sack avoidance percentage equals one hundred times recorded dropbacks minus recorded sacks, divided by recorded dropbacks.">Without a sack (%) = 100 × (dropbacks − sacks) ÷ dropbacks.</p>

The denominator is recorded dropbacks, not pressures or included throws. Interceptions, incompletions and low-value escapes can count as avoiding a sack. Protection and receiver availability matter. This is not measured pressure escape or pocket presence. Individual uncertainty was not estimated. [Play definitions](#nfl-definition-populations) · [Limitations](#nfl-limitations).

<h2 id="nfl-definition-populations" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>Who and which plays are included?</h2>

The denominator is the count you divide by; it determines which plays the statistic describes.

<h3 id="nfl-definition-annual" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>Annual eligibility and source coverage</h3>

The annual comparison contains **560 qualifying player-seasons across 19 exported seasons from 2006 through 2025**. Each player qualifies within that season with at least 200 frozen included throws and ten included-throw games. Team changes pool under the same recorded GSIS player ID. No adjacent-season appearance is required. These are recorded passers, not a position-certified roster or every NFL quarterback; completion-model availability and exposure affect who qualifies. The postseason is excluded.

The catalog retains all 20 years. **2011 is source-blocked** because unresolved duplicate recorded events and conflicting air yards prevent an accepted input. It contributes no annual or depth rows. Years outside 2006–2025 have no accepted data in this comparison. A selected player-year without an eligible record stays unavailable; the display does not substitute a nearby year, another player or zero.

The original repeatability evidence uses a separate, fixed group of 20 surviving passer IDs with at least 200 included throws and ten included-throw games in **both** 2023 and 2024. Its 40 annual records and ten study records remain labeled as the original cohort. The new annual selection does not extend those repeatability findings to 560 player-seasons. Only the original 40 records supply audited scramble values.

- **Included throws (E):** the accepted recorded pass-outcome population, with finite supplied completion probability and CPOE, completion probability between zero and one, air yards at least −15 and below 70, observed pass location and the accepted receiver-name or receiver-ID condition. Frozen eligibility checks the CPOE identity. CPOE and completion share these exact rows; there is no FTN filter or missing-as-zero rule. The public audit code specifies the full pass-outcome/key rules.
- **Recorded dropbacks (D):** recorded dropbacks on pass/run play types, with spike, kneel and two-point flags zero. Passes/sacks use the raw passer ID; scramble-runs use the raw rusher ID. Played penalties remain. Other play types and no-play records lie outside this population. It has no completion, completion-probability or included-throw-game filter, and may differ from another site's dropback rule.
- **Scrambles (S):** the recorded scramble-run subset of dropbacks. These counts describe opportunities, not pressures escaped.
- **Other recorded runs (R), original 2023/2024 cohort only:** recorded non-scramble runs by a known member of that fixed group, excluding kneels and conversions. They include aborted plays and laterals. Non-scramble does not establish designed-run intent or athletic mobility.
- **Included QB plays (O), original 2023/2024 cohort only:** the disjoint union of recorded dropbacks and other recorded runs. Scrambles already belong to dropbacks. This is a selected recorded-opportunity population, not every snap or offensive play.

Games count distinct player games containing the named type of record. Role games contain at least one event of that run type. Summed player-game counts are not distinct NFL games. [Full audit and reproduction code](https://github.com/nanu731/nfl-qb-eval).

<h2 id="nfl-method-models" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>How the models work</h2>

The data provider supplies completion chances and expected-points values. This project summarizes recorded outcomes using those values. The original fixed-cohort studies examine associations across game groups and seasons; the wider annual and depth data provide descriptive comparisons.

The supplied completion model uses recorded throw/context information. The inspected upstream implementation includes depth/location, down/distance and a QB-hit field; these are not all independent of QB decisions. The project neither fits nor retrains this model. The inspected releases do not establish which model/training vintage generated these supplied values, their out-of-sample status or their calibration in each era. An asset update date does not identify the generating model. [Completion model implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nflverse/nflfastR/master/R/helper_add_cp_cpoe.R).

The upstream expected-points model uses game-state inputs such as field position, down/distance and time left. It estimates scoring-event probabilities and converts them to expected points. EPA then values the resulting play. The project uses supplied raw EPA, not receiver-fumble-adjusted QB EPA, and fits no expected-points model. The generating expected-points model and training vintage remain unknown for these snapshots. Modeled game-state adjustment does not isolate protection, receivers or coaching. Comparing seasons with different football conditions and unknown model calibration does not produce an era-neutral ability comparison. [Official expected-points explanation](https://nflfastr.com/reference/calculate_expected_points.html) · [Versioned EPA implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nflverse/nflfastR/v5.2.0/R/helper_add_ep_wp.R).

<h2 id="nfl-method-uncertainty" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>What the game-based ranges mean</h2>

An approximate range based on game-to-game variation, under the study's assumptions. Here, the bars describe approximate uncertainty around each summary, not the spread of game performances. These conditional ranges are not probabilities for true talent, predictions for next season or bounds on every source of uncertainty.

The annual EPA range removes one complete player dropback game at a time and recalculates the pooled EPA/dropback ratio, holding qualification fixed. It uses all games containing that player's recorded dropbacks. The original run-contribution ranges use all combined-opportunity games, including games with zero events of the named run role.

**Depth ranges use all of the player's included-throw games in that season**, including games with zero throws in the named depth bin. Let `G` be that full game count, `n_bg` the bin throws in game `g`, and `s_bg` their sum of supplied CPOE values. The full estimate is `theta_b = sum_g(s_bg) / sum_g(n_bg)`. The leave-one-game-out estimate is `theta_b[-g] = (sum_h(s_bh) − s_bg) / (N_b − n_bg)`. A zero-bin game remains a deletion with no removed bin throws; the positive-bin-game count does not replace `G`.

Every deletion must retain a positive denominator and finite estimate. The analysis neither drops failed replicates nor requalifies the player after deletion. An empty bin leaves the estimate unavailable; an invalid deletion leaves the range unavailable, with an explicit status. For valid deletions, `m = sum_g(theta[-g]) / G`, `V = (G−1)/G × sum_g((theta[-g]−m)^2)`, and the displayed range is `theta ± 1.96 × sqrt(V)`. This is an approximate marginal 95% conditional game-deletion sensitivity, not the minimum-to-maximum deletion range.

A collapsed range means no observed deletion variation, not certainty. Whole-season CPOE, completion rate and individual sack avoidance have no supplied individual range here. The three depth ranges do **not** provide simultaneous 95% coverage. Marginal ranges neither test differences between depth bins, players or years nor establish rank certainty; overlap and non-overlap do not supply those missing tests.

The calculation assumes approximately independent game shocks, stable opportunity sampling and a useful normal approximation, conditional on fixed qualification and observed support. It omits uncertainty from selection, serial injuries, receivers, protection, coverage, scheme, source labels and the supplied models' training/calibration. Unknown model vintage and era changes preclude talent-confidence, causal, era-neutral or forecasting interpretations.

<h2 id="nfl-definition-runs" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>Running values in the complete table</h2>

These running summaries cover only the original 40 audited 2023/2024 records. Outside that lookup, the comparison shows no audited scramble-value record; it does not substitute scramble rate, annual dropbacks or an uncalculated contribution.

Value from scrambles is scramble EPA spread over all included dropbacks and other recorded player runs. Other recorded run value spreads non-scramble-run EPA over those same included plays. These contributions combine usage with shared value; more usage is not universally better.

Formulas: `C_S = sum_S(EPA)/N_O`; `C_R = sum_R(EPA)/N_O`. Shares are `100 × N_S/N_O` and `100 × N_R/N_O`, percent. EPA per run divides each role's EPA by that role's count and remains a diagnostic with no supplied interval or repeatability test. A fully observed absent role has zero share and contribution, with unavailable per-run EPA. Zero contribution can also result from positive and negative EPA cancelling; inspect the counts. Missing evidence never becomes zero. Adding these contributions to EPA per dropback would mix denominators and double-count scrambles.

<h2 id="nfl-research-question" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>Research question</h2>

Can public NFL data help describe repeatable quarterback execution with less dependence on supporting circumstances?

<h2 id="nfl-method-repeatability" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>What repeatability measures</h2>

The findings below belong to the original fixed 20-passer 2023/2024 cohort and its ten study records. The new 560-player-season annual data and depth summaries supply no new repeatability test.

Repeatability asks whether players with higher values in one group also tended to have higher values in the other. Across-passer association does not measure a selected player's personal consistency, absolute agreement, retained-skill percentage or forecast accuracy.

Correlation is `r = sum_i[(x_i−mean(x)) × (y_i−mean(y))] / sqrt(sum_i(x_i−mean(x))² × sum_i(y_i−mean(y))²)` across equally weighted passers. Their pooled summaries supply x/y. Near +1 means strong positive linear alignment, not identical values. Zero means no linear association, not zero talent. Constant values make correlation undefined.

The sack study's 2023 halves alternate each player's date/game-ID-ordered dropback games; run halves alternate combined-opportunity games. Whole player-games stay intact. Annual comparisons use full 2023/2024 summaries. These shared/overlapping studies are not independent replications and do not directly test future performance.

Annual CPOE association was **0.507**, versus **0.452** for completion percentage on the same throws. Their difference was **0.055**, with a conditional interval from **−0.274 to 0.384**. Omitting one paired player reverses that difference. The evidence does not establish CPOE superiority. The paired correlation difference is dimensionless, not a percentage-point completion gap.

Recorded sack avoidance had a primary interleaved association of **0.265 [−0.289,0.685]**. Chronological and annual results answer separate questions; all three remain in the study table.

Primary scramble-contribution association was **0.313 [−0.190,0.686]**; other-run association was **0.697 [0.004,0.938]**. Annual associations were **0.727 [0.226,0.924]** and **0.429 [−0.080,0.761]**. All four endpoints remain in the study table.

Study ranges remove a whole contributing NFL game across both teams and selected players, keeping membership/maps fixed. Sack/run correlations use Fisher transforms and each season's own deletion mean. Annual CPOE retains its accepted full-estimate centering; the paired difference uses the same game deletions for both metrics. These approximate conditional ranges assume approximately independent game shocks and suitable smooth-statistic behavior. The 223 + 208 annual game units do not form 431 independent quarterback pairs or repeated validation studies.

<h2 id="nfl-limitations" tabIndex={-1} data-reference-target>What these comparisons cannot tell us</h2>

Receiver/defender actions, target choices and unmeasured circumstances remain. Protection, receivers and coaching are not isolated. These summaries do not establish precise placement, pocket awareness, designed rushing, athletic mobility, overall QB quality or forecasts. Arm strength, processing and a support-adjusted overall score remain unavailable.

The annual display exports qualifying records from 19 seasons within 2006–2025; unresolved source conflicts keep 2011 visible and blocked. Eligibility selects for recorded exposure and completion-model availability, and the original studies add survival across two years. Source coverage, overlap, persistent support and conditional uncertainty limit interpretation. Unknown generating model vintages and era changes prevent era-neutral skill claims. The depth residuals share outcomes and context; they do not validate three isolated accuracy traits. No rank, percentile, win count or blended score combines the radar spokes or optional comparisons.
